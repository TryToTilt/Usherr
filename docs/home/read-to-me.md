# Read-to-Me

**Read to me** turns an ebook into an audiobook with a computer voice, on your
own computer. It is on every ebook's page (an EPUB), for accounts that may
manage the library. The finished audiobook goes in your **Audiobooks** library
as one M4B file with chapters and the book's cover, so it plays in Usherr's
player, in Audiobookshelf and in phone apps, with bookmarks. It is marked
*Read by Usherr · the voice's name*, so it is never taken for a real narrator.

## The Read-to-Me page

**Read to me** on an ebook's page, and **Read-to-Me** at the top of the Ebooks
library, open the Read-to-Me page. From the Library it starts by asking which
book (only downloaded EPUBs can be read).

The page has six steps, each a box in its own colour: **Book** (blue),
**Narrator** (purple), **Cast** (pink), **Sound** (amber), **Words** (green)
and **Make it**. A step folds to one line once it is done, and **Open** or
the coloured dots at the top bring it back. **?** next to a step, and the
small **i** next to an option, say what it is for.

- **Simple** shows only Book, Narrator and Make it, with the usual settings
  for the rest. **Full** shows all six.
- **Guide me** lights one step at a time and dims the others; **Next** at the
  bottom of a step folds it and opens the next one. It is on until the first
  audiobook has been made, and the choice is kept in the browser.
- The bar at the bottom stays in sight: the book, the voice, **Hear a sample**
  (about 200 words of the book in the settings you picked) and **Make the
  audiobook**.
- Below the steps, **Your Read-to-Me** has what is being made, what was made,
  the engines, the voices and the words said your way in every book, each
  folded up until you open it.

## Making one

1. Open an ebook's page and press **Read to me**.
2. Pick a voice in **Narrator**. **▶** on each voice plays a short sample of it
   (made once, then kept). The everyday voices come first, best graded first.
3. Set the speed in **Sound** (0.8× to 1.3×), and press **Hear a sample** to
   hear about 200 words in that voice before you commit.
4. Press **Make the audiobook**. The **Make it** step says how long the book is and how
   long making it should take, and while it works it shows the chapter it is
   on, how far it is and about how long is left. **Stop making it** cancels.
   You can leave the page: the job carries on, and the audiobook appears in
   your library when it is done.

While it is being made, **Listen while it is being made** (in **Make it** and
under **Your Read-to-Me**) plays any chapter already spoken, so you can start the
book before it is finished.

When it is done (or if it couldn't be), Usherr can send a notice: tick
**Audiobooks made** on a notification target (Settings > Notifications). It
says the voice, the length, and how many lines to check, if any.

One audiobook is made at a time; the others wait their turn (up to twelve).
A job stopped by a restart carries on where it was: what is already spoken is
kept until the audiobook is finished.

## Presets

A preset is a named way of reading, like *Warm narrator* or *Thriller*: the
voice (or a mix), the speed, the pauses, and whether the title and the
chapter titles are read. The chapters you pick stay with the book.

- **Save these settings as a preset** in the **Make it** step. Saving
  under a name that is already there updates that preset. Tick **Use it by
  default** to have the page start with it.
- **Preset** in the **Narrator** step switches to one.
- The **Voices** part of **Your Read-to-Me** lists them with **Hear**, **Make
  it the default** and **Delete**.

Presets are shared by everyone in the household who can make audiobooks.

## Mixing two voices

With an everyday (Kokoro) voice picked, **Mix in a second voice** blends it
with another: pick the second voice and slide the balance (from 90/10 to
10/90). **Hear the mix** plays a sample. The audiobook is marked with both,
*Read by Usherr · Heart 70% + Bella 30%*. A mix can be saved in a preset.

## What is read

The **Book** step chooses:

- **Start with the title and the author** (on unless you turn it off).
- **Read each chapter's title** (on unless you turn it off). "Chapter IV" is
  read "Chapter four".
- **Pauses**: after a paragraph (0.6 s unless you change it), at a scene
  break (1.5 s) and between chapters (2.5 s). **The usual pauses** puts them
  back.
- **The chapters**: all of them, or only the ones you tick. The estimate
  follows what you pick, and the preview starts at the first one picked.

The contents, the copyright page, the acknowledgments and other pages nobody
wants read aloud are left out by themselves.

## Reading like a narrator

The text is made ready before a voice reads it:

- Titles and abbreviations in full: *Dr.* "Doctor", *Mr.* "Mister", *St. James*
  "Saint James", *e.g.* "for example".
- Numbers in words: *1999* "nineteen ninety-nine", *the 3rd* "the third",
  *$5* "five dollars", *40%* "forty percent", *10:05* "ten oh five", *the
  1990s* "the nineteen nineties". Decimals, codes like *B12*, and numbers of a
  million or more are left to the voice.
- Dashes and ellipses become pauses; footnote markers are dropped.
- Pauses like a narrator's: a short one between sentences, longer after a
  paragraph, longer still at a scene break (*** or a line across the page),
  and the longest between chapters.
- The finished audiobook is levelled to one loudness, so no chapter is
  quieter than the next.

### How words are said

Names and made-up words a voice gets wrong can be written the way they
sound: *Hermione* → *her-MY-oh-nee*. The **Words** step has
two lists, **In this book** and **In every book** (the book's own list wins).
The every-book list is also under **Your Read-to-Me**. A word matches as a whole
word, in any case.

## Voices for the dialogue (full cast)

**Voices for the dialogue** in the **Cast** step chooses how speech in quotation
marks is read:

- **One voice reads it all** (as before).
- **Narrator and a dialogue voice**: everything in quotation marks in a second
  voice; *said Tom* stays with the narrator.
- **A voice for each character**: Usherr finds the book's characters and how
  many lines each has, and you give the ones you want a voice of their own.
  The rest are read by **Everyone else**, or by **Other men** and **Other
  women** when it is known whether the speaker is a man or a woman. A
  first-person narrator's own lines stay in the narrator's voice unless you
  give "I" a voice.

Any voice can be used, Kokoro or Chatterbox (with Chatterbox voices the book
takes longer). The cast is kept with the book, so a remake reads it the same
way. It isn't part of a preset (it belongs to one book).

### Who says each line

Usherr works it out from the book's own words, in this order:

1. what the book says: *"Run," Tom said*, *said Tom*, *Tom said, "Run"*, and
   *she said* (at least a woman);
2. what the person does: a paragraph that starts *Tom grinned.* and has a
   quote is Tom's;
3. the AI, when you tick **Ask the AI about lines the book doesn't tag** (or
   press **Ask it now** to see the result before making the book): one
   request a chapter, to the AI set up in Settings > Extras > AI, done once
   and kept. It also says who is a man or a woman;
4. turns: in an exchange between two people, an untagged line is the next
   one's;
5. what you set by hand, which always wins.

A name has to come up at least twice to be a character. The **Cast** step says for
how many lines the speaker is known. **Check the lines** shows a chapter's
lines with who says each and how that was found (*the book says*, *the AI*,
*their turn*...); type a name to give a line to someone else, and **Undo** to
take that back.

## How Chatterbox reads

With a Chatterbox voice (or one in the cast), **How Chatterbox reads** in the
**Sound** step has its dials:

- **Emotion**: flat at the low end, dramatic at the high end (0.5 unless you
  change it). Above about 1 it can overact.
- **Pacing**: lower is slower and more deliberate (0.5). With more emotion, a
  lower pacing keeps it steady; about 0.7 emotion and 0.3 pacing is a good
  start for a dramatic reading.
- **Variety**: how much each line differs from the last (0.8). Too high and
  it can mumble.
- **Keep this take**: Chatterbox reads a line a little differently each time.
  A kept take (a number) reads the same text the same way every time, so the
  book reads the way the preview did, and a remake reads it the same way
  again. **Another take** tries a new one.

Turbo has no emotion or pacing dial (they are greyed out with Turbo); it
takes the variety and the take. **The usual way** puts them all back. The
dials and the take are part of a preset.

## Comparing two ways of reading

**Compare two ways of reading** in the **Sound** step reads the same 60 words of the
book two ways: **A** is the settings above, **B** a preset, or another take of
the same settings. **Hear A** and **Hear B** play them (each is made once).
**Keep take B** (or **Keep take A**) keeps the one you liked; **Use B** switches
to the preset.

When Chatterbox has to be started first, the page says so (about a minute
the first time) instead of looking stuck.

## Copied voices

**Library > Ebooks > Read-to-Me > Voices** has the **Copied voices**: a short
recording of one person speaking becomes a Chatterbox voice.

1. **Copy a voice**: a name, a recording (WAV, MP3, M4A or FLAC; 10 to 20
   seconds of one person speaking clearly, no music or other voices), and the
   box that says you have the right to use the voice: your own, or with the
   speaker's permission.
2. Usherr makes it ready: mono, the silence at the start cut, even loudness,
   the first 20 seconds kept. Less than 5 seconds of sound is refused.
3. The voice is in every voice list from then on, the cast too. **Hear it**
   reads a sentence in it; **The recording** plays what it was copied from;
   **Delete** deletes it (an administrator or the person who copied it).

Usherr keeps the recording and gives it to Chatterbox (standard or Turbo) the
first time that model reads with it, so a copied voice can be picked while
Chatterbox is stopped.

A WAV put straight into the voices folder (`readtome/voices` in Usherr's
config folder) is taken in the same way the next time the voices are listed,
named after the file: `UK woman - Ruth.wav` becomes **UK woman - Ruth**. A file
with too little sound is renamed `.unusable` and left alone. Taking a file
out of the folder deletes the voice it made. Public-domain
recordings, such as LibriVox's audiobooks, are a good source of voices.

## Lines read badly

Now and then Chatterbox repeats itself, babbles or leaves words out. Usherr
times every line it reads against its words: a line far too long or far too
short for them is read again (another take, up to three) and the best one
kept. One that still doesn't fit is marked: the audiobook's entry says
**Lines to check (2)**. (Kokoro reads what it is given; its lines aren't
checked.)

## Reading a line or a chapter again

A made audiobook keeps what it was made of for 30 days: its chapters (without
loss) and where each line is in them. **Lines** (or **Lines to check**) on its
entry, in **Make it** or under **Your Read-to-Me**, shows them:

- the lines to check first, then any chapter's lines; **▶** plays a line as
  it is in the audiobook;
- **Another take** reads a line again; **Other words** reads it as you type it
  (a name spelled out, a word the ebook has wrong);
- **Read the chapter again** reads the whole chapter again, from the ebook as
  it reads now (with its text put right, below).

Only that line or chapter is spoken; the audiobook is then put together anew
from the lossless chapters, so the rest loses nothing, and it takes the old
one's place once it is done (a few minutes for a long book). It waits its
turn with the other jobs.

The kept makings take about 100 MB an hour of audio. **Let go of it now**
frees the space sooner; the audiobook stays, and a remake still works. They
also go when the audiobook is deleted or remade.

## Putting a chapter's text right

In **What is read**, **Text** next to a chapter opens the words Read-to-Me
will read: change them (a blank line starts a paragraph, \*\*\* a scene
break) and **Save**. The chapter is marked *your text*, and everything reads
it that way: the preview, the audiobook, a chapter read again. **Back to the
ebook's text** undoes it. The ebook file is never changed; if the ebook itself
changes, the edit no longer applies.

## Remaking or deleting one

When Read-to-Me has made a book, its **Make it** step says so (**Already made:** the
voice and the date) with three buttons:

- **Play** opens it in the player.
- **Remake** makes it again: pick another voice or speed and press **Remake
  the audiobook**. The one you have stays playable while the new one is made,
  and the new one takes its place only once it is finished. Stopping the
  remake leaves the old one as it was.
- **Delete** deletes the audiobook (it asks first). The ebook stays.

Remake and Delete are for an administrator or the person who made it, and
only for audiobooks Read-to-Me made. A book with a real audiobook (one that
came from anywhere else) is never remade or deleted from here. The ebook is
never touched.

## The Read-to-Me tab

**Library > Ebooks > Read-to-Me** has it all in one place:

- **Being made**: each job with its chapter, how far it is and about how long
  is left, and **Stop**. A job that didn't finish says why.
- **Made by Read-to-Me**: every audiobook it made, with the voice, the speed,
  the date and the size, and **Play**, **Lines**, **Remake** and **Delete**.
- **Engines**: Kokoro, Chatterbox and Chatterbox Turbo, whether each runs, and
  (with the runner) **Start** and **Stop**.
- **Voices**: the presets and the copied voices. **How words are said**: the
  every-book list.

The tab is there for accounts that may manage the library, once a voice
engine is set up.

## The voices

- **Kokoro** is the everyday narrator: about 70 voices (the English ones are
  offered), fast (an hour of speech in a few minutes on a graphics card) and
  light on memory. It stays on.
- **Chatterbox** is more expressive and can copy a voice from a short sample.
  It is slower (making an hour of speech takes about an hour and ten minutes on
  an RTX 3070) and takes about 3.5 GB of graphics memory, so it doesn't run
  all the time: with the runner (below) Usherr starts it when a job needs it
  and stops it again when the queue is empty; without it, it is started by
  hand and its voices are greyed out while it is off. It can copy a voice
  (see **Copied voices** above).
- **Chatterbox Turbo** is a faster Chatterbox (about twice as fast as the
  speech is long on an RTX 3070, so an hour of speech in about half an hour),
  a little flatter, without the emotion dial. It runs in place of Chatterbox,
  on the same address: in the **Narrator** step, **Standard** or **Turbo** picks the one
  a book is read with, and the runner starts that one (stopping the other).
  Settings chooses the household's usual one. Without the runner, stop one and
  start the other by hand (`chatterbox-turbo` in the compose file,
  `--profile turbo`). Both share the copied voices.

## Setting it up

Both voices run as containers next to Usherr, on a computer with an NVIDIA
graphics card (8 GB is enough; the card is shared with whatever else uses it).

1. Copy `docker-compose.yml` and `chatterbox-fix.Dockerfile` from
   [docs/home/read-to-me](read-to-me/docker-compose.yml) into a folder of its own, for
   example `E:\docker\read-to-me`.
2. Start Kokoro: `docker compose up -d`. It downloads its image and model the first
   time. Its port is 8881 here (8880 is often taken).
3. For Chatterbox, clone its API once and build it (it has no ready-made image), then
   start it when you want it:

   ```
   git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/travisvn/chatterbox-tts-api chatterbox-src
   docker build -f chatterbox-src/docker/Dockerfile.uv.gpu -t read-to-me-chatterbox-base chatterbox-src
   docker compose --profile chatterbox up -d
   docker compose --profile chatterbox stop chatterbox     (when you are done)
   ```

   The build is large (about 15 GB) and the first start downloads the model. The
   small `chatterbox-fix.Dockerfile` on top pins an older `setuptools`, without
   which Chatterbox's watermark library doesn't load and the model fails to start.
4. In Usherr: **Settings → Extras → Read-to-Me**: enter Kokoro's address (from
   Usherr's container: `http://host.docker.internal:8881`) and Chatterbox's
   (`http://host.docker.internal:4123`), press **Save**, then **Test Kokoro**.
5. The runner (optional, so Usherr can start and stop Chatterbox itself): copy
   the `runner` folder too, put a long random token in a `.env` file next to the
   compose file (`RUNNER_TOKEN=...`, 24 characters or more, never in a
   repository), run `docker compose create chatterbox chatterbox-turbo` once (the
   runner starts containers, it doesn't make them) and `docker compose up -d
   runner`. In Usherr enter its address (`http://host.docker.internal:4130`) and
   the same token, then **Test the runner**.

   The runner has the Docker socket, but answers only for `kokoro`,
   `chatterbox` and `chatterbox-turbo`, only with the token, and only start,
   stop and status. Usherr stops only what it started itself: Chatterbox goes
   two minutes after its last piece when nothing else is queued, and while a
   job has been held back (night only, something playing) for ten minutes.

## When it works

- **Make audiobooks at night only** (in the same card) holds jobs back outside two
  times of day (for example 23:00 to 07:00), so the graphics card is free in the
  evening.
- A job also waits, and says so, **while something is playing** in Usherr's player or
  on Jellyfin or Emby (a paused picture doesn't count), and goes on when it stops.

## What it needs

The ebook must be an EPUB in the library. Chapters come from the book's table of
contents; the cover page, the copyright page, the table of contents and pages
with next to nothing on them are left out, and footnote marks are not read. Each
chapter's title is spoken first, and the book's title and author at the start.
